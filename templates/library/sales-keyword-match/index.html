Why it's different

Not your usual retinol.

Most retinol serums hit your skin all at once. That's why first-timers get dry patches and give up by week two.

This one wraps a gentle retinal in tiny lipid capsules that open through the night, with ceramides that keep your skin barrier calm while it works.

How it works

Three steps, once a night.

1

Cleanse and pat dry

Start on clean, completely dry skin. Damp skin takes in retinoids faster than it needs to.

2

Press in two drops

Warm two drops between your fingertips and press them over your face, avoiding the eye area.

3

Wake up, then SPF

Your moisturiser in the morning, then sunscreen. Start every other night for the first two weeks.

The key ingredient

Encapsulated retinal.

Retinal is one step closer to the form your skin actually uses, so a lower dose does the work. The capsule sets the pace, releasing it slowly over the night instead of all at once.

Questions

Everything you want to know.

Is it okay for sensitive skin?

Yes. The retinal is released slowly and paired with ceramides and bisabolol, and the formula has no fragrance or essential oils. Start every other night for the first two weeks.

How often should I use it?

Every other night for two weeks, then nightly if your skin feels comfortable. Two drops cover the whole face.

Can I use it with vitamin C or exfoliating acids?

Use vitamin C in the morning and this serum at night. Skip strong acid exfoliants on the same night while your skin gets used to it.

When will I see a difference?

Most people say their skin feels softer in the first week and looks smoother by week four. Give it a full eight weeks for tone.

Do I need sunscreen?

Yes. Retinoids make skin more sensitive to the sun, so wear SPF 30 or higher every morning.

Can I use it while pregnant or breastfeeding?

Retinoids aren't recommended during pregnancy or breastfeeding. Please ask your doctor first.

What if it doesn't suit me?

You have 60 nights to try it. If you're not happy, we'll refund you, even if the bottle is empty.
